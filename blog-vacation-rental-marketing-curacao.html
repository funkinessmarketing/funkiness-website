Every vacation rental on Curaçao looks the same online.
Here's how you don't.

Marketing

25 August 2026

5 min read

← All posts

Open Airbnb or Booking.com and search for a vacation rental on Curaçao. Scroll for two minutes. Notice how many listings say some version of "your home away from home," show the same pool-at-sunset photo, and describe the property instead of the guest who'd actually book it.

That's the real competition. Not the villa three doors down. The scroll itself.

What actually makes a guest scroll past your listing

It's rarely the property. Most vacation rentals on Curaçao are genuinely nice. What loses the booking is a listing that reads like every other listing: generic language, photos that don't show what a guest actually needs to know, and a description written for "everyone," which means it speaks to no one in particular.

Guests skim. They're comparing you against a dozen open tabs. A listing that sounds like it was written for a brochure gets skimmed too, and skimmed means skipped.

Your photos do more work than your price

Price gets you into the search results. Photos get you the click. A few things that consistently make a difference: shoot at the time of day a guest will actually use the space, not just golden hour. Show the walk from the door to the beach or the pool, not just the destination. Show room sizes honestly, wide shots that make a small room look staged read as misleading once a guest arrives, and that shows up in reviews.

Guests aren't looking for perfect. They're looking for accurate. Accurate photos set expectations a real stay can actually meet.

Write for one specific guest, not "everyone"

A couple celebrating an anniversary, a family with young kids, and a remote worker staying a month all want different things from the same property, and they search with different words. "Quiet and private" pulls one of those guests in. "Steps from the beach, kid-friendly pool fence" pulls in another. Trying to appeal to all three in one description usually appeals to none of them strongly enough to book.

Pick the guest your property genuinely suits best, and write the whole listing for that one person. It's a smaller net, but a much stronger pull.

The best-performing listings on Curaçao aren't always the biggest villas. They're the ones that tell a guest exactly who they're for, before that guest even opens the photos.

Reviews are your second listing

Guests read reviews before they read your description. And they read your responses to reviews just as closely, especially the critical ones. A calm, specific, non-defensive response to a bad review often reassures a future guest more than another five-star review does. It shows there's a real person managing the property who takes it seriously.

Respond quickly, acknowledge what actually happened, and skip the public back-and-forth. That's it. That's the whole strategy, and almost no one does it consistently.

Turning one-time guests into repeat, direct bookings

Every platform booking comes with a commission. Every direct rebooking doesn't. The guests most likely to book directly next time are the ones who already stayed with you and had a good experience, so the follow-up after checkout matters more than most owners treat it. A short, genuine thank-you message, sent the way the platform allows, keeps the door open for next time without ever pressuring anyone during an active booking.

Over a year, that's the difference between rebuilding your guest list every season and having one that grows on its own.
